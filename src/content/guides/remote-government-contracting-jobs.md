Quick clarification before anything else, because this trips people up constantly: "Contracting" as a federal job category is not the same thing as working *for* a government contractor company. This guide is about the former — federal employees whose job is to manage and administer government contracts. If you're looking for jobs at companies like Leidos, CACI, or GDIT that do contract work for the government, see our [contractor companies page](/companies) instead.

## What the Contracting occupational series actually does

Federal contracting specialists and contracting officers manage the government's side of procurement — writing and issuing solicitations, evaluating proposals from companies competing for government work, negotiating contract terms, administering contracts once awarded, and ensuring compliance with federal acquisition regulations throughout a contract's life. It's a career field built around procurement law, negotiation, and program oversight rather than the delivery of the underlying goods or services being contracted for.

## Why this category suits remote and telework work well

Contracting work is fundamentally document- and process-driven — reviewing proposals, drafting contract language, tracking compliance, and communicating with contractors and internal stakeholders. Very little of it requires being physically present at a specific location, which makes it one of the more consistently telework-friendly categories in federal government, and a reasonable source of fully remote postings as well.

## What qualifications typically look like

Federal contracting positions generally require:

- Specific coursework or experience requirements defined under federal contracting qualification standards (often referred to by their regulatory basis — check the specific announcement)
- Increasing levels of contracting officer warrant authority at higher grades, which typically requires documented experience managing contracts of increasing dollar value and complexity
- Familiarity with the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) and agency-specific acquisition procedures

If you have private-sector procurement, purchasing, or vendor management experience, it can translate to federal contracting roles, but the qualification standards are specific — read the announcement's qualifications section carefully rather than assuming general procurement background is automatically sufficient.

## The career path

Federal contracting has a defined progression — contract specialist roles building toward contracting officer positions with increasing warrant authority (the dollar value of contracts you're authorized to sign off on). This makes it a career field with a relatively clear trajectory compared to some other federal occupational series, which some job seekers find appealing for long-term planning.
