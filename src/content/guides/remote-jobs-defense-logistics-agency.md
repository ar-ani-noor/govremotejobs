The Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) manages the global supply chain for the US military — everything from food and fuel to spare parts and construction materials — and it's a consistent source of remote and telework-eligible civilian positions, particularly in logistics, supply chain, and contracting fields.

## What DLA does

DLA is the primary logistics combat support agency for the Department of Defense, responsible for sourcing, storing, and distributing the huge range of supplies the military depends on. It's overwhelmingly a civilian-staffed organization built around supply chain management, procurement, logistics planning, and distribution operations rather than direct military functions.

## Why this agency has meaningful remote/telework potential

Modern logistics and supply chain work is heavily data- and systems-driven — demand forecasting, inventory management, procurement, contract administration, and distribution network analysis all happen substantially through information systems rather than requiring constant physical presence at a warehouse or distribution point. This makes DLA a reasonable source of telework-eligible roles, and occasionally fully remote ones, for the right specialties.

## Typical roles

Common position types include: logistics management specialists, supply chain analysts, contract specialists and contracting officers, IT specialists supporting DLA's logistics systems, and program and management analysts. If your background is in supply chain, procurement, or logistics — in any industry — this is worth checking regardless of whether you've worked in defense or government before, since these skills transfer directly.

## What to check before applying

As with any defense-adjacent agency, some DLA positions involve access to sensitive logistics or supply chain information relevant to military readiness, which can carry clearance or suitability requirements — check each announcement's security requirements individually. DLA operates across numerous physical locations (distribution centers, regional commands, headquarters), so confirm the specific duty station and telework terms for any posting rather than assuming based on the agency generally.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need military or defense industry experience to apply to DLA?** No — DLA is a civilian-staffed logistics organization, and most positions don't require prior military service or defense-sector background. Relevant private-sector supply chain, procurement, or logistics experience transfers directly; describe it in federal-resume terms (see our [federal resume guide](/guides/how-to-write-a-federal-resume)) rather than assuming it won't count because it wasn't earned in government.

**Are DLA telework arrangements likely to change?** Telework policy across the federal government has shifted meaningfully in recent years and can change again with future policy decisions. Treat any specific announcement's stated telework terms as current at the time of posting, not as a permanent guarantee, and revisit your expectations if you're weighing a long-term relocation decision around it.

**How does DLA compare to logistics roles at federal contractors?** Direct DLA employment carries standard federal benefits and hiring timelines (see our [benefits guide](/guides/federal-benefits-valued-in-dollars) and [timeline guide](/guides/usajobs-application-timeline)); contractor logistics roles — including at companies we track like [Leidos](/guides/remote-jobs-leidos) — typically hire faster and may offer more remote flexibility, at the tradeoff of different job security and benefits structures.
