These two words get used loosely in everyday conversation, but in federal hiring they mean specifically different things — and confusing them is the single most common way job seekers waste applications on positions that don't actually fit what they're looking for.

## Remote: no duty station, period

A fully remote position has no assigned physical office. You are not expected to commute anywhere, ever, as a condition of the job. This is the smallest category of federal jobs by far — inventory shrank sharply after the January 2025 return-to-office order, and it remains thin.

## Telework eligible: tied to an office, with flexibility

A telework-eligible position has a real duty station — a specific federal building somewhere — but the agency permits the employee to work from home some portion of the time. This could mean one day a week, several days a week, or nearly full-time in "maximum telework" arrangements at some agencies, but the position is still legally anchored to that office, and you may be required to report there on short notice, for specific meetings, or under a schedule that can change with policy.

## Why the distinction actually matters

If you're not willing or able to relocate near a specific city, a telework-eligible listing may not work for you even if the day-to-day experience sounds remote — you're still expected to live within commuting distance of the duty station, and telework arrangements can be reduced or reversed by policy changes with far less notice than you'd get from a private employer changing its remote policy.

Conversely, if you're flexible on location and mainly want more open pay, benefits, or mission-driven work, telework-eligible roles represent a dramatically larger pool of opportunities than fully remote ones — several thousand telework-eligible listings exist at any given time on this site alone, against a few dozen fully remote ones.

## How to tell which one you're looking at

Every listing on USAJOBS — and on this site — states the work location policy explicitly. We tag every job as either "Fully remote" or "Telework eligible" and never blur the two into a single "remote-friendly" category, because the practical difference for your life (do you need to live near a specific city or not) is too significant to soften.

## Which should you target?

If location flexibility is non-negotiable for you, filter for [fully remote](/remote) only, understand the pool is small, and be patient — check back regularly, since new fully-remote postings appear infrequently and close quickly.

If you have some flexibility about where you live, or you're already near a major federal hub, [telework-eligible](/telework) roles open up a much larger set of real opportunities, often with meaningful in-office flexibility in practice even if the job isn't formally "remote."

Either way, read the position's location and telework terms directly in the announcement before you apply — don't rely on the job title or a general impression from the summary.
