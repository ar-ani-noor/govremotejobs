If you work in IT or cybersecurity, you're looking at the single largest occupational category in remote and telework-eligible government work — and it's not close. Across the listings we track, Information Technology Management consistently ranks as the biggest category by volume.

## Why IT dominates this space

A few structural reasons make technology roles a natural fit for remote and telework arrangements, and a hiring priority for agencies broadly:

- **The work is inherently portable.** Writing code, managing systems, and analyzing security logs doesn't require physical presence at a specific building the way many operational government roles do.
- **Government-wide cybersecurity investment has grown substantially**, driven by an increasing volume of threats against federal systems — this has translated into sustained hiring demand across nearly every agency, not just defense- and intelligence-focused ones.
- **The federal government competes directly with the private sector for the same technical talent pool**, and remote/telework flexibility is one of the more cost-effective ways agencies can compete on total offer attractiveness without simply outbidding private salaries.

## What roles fall under this category

"Information Technology Management" as a federal occupational series covers a wide range of specific work: systems administration, network engineering, cybersecurity and information assurance, software development, database administration, IT project management, and enterprise architecture, among others. If your background is in any of these, search broadly rather than assuming only postings with your exact job title apply to you — federal position titles don't always map cleanly onto private-sector titles.

## Security clearances: read this carefully

A meaningful share of federal and federal-contractor IT and cybersecurity positions require a security clearance, particularly anything touching defense, intelligence, or classified systems. Clearance requirements vary enormously — some roles need none, some need a basic public trust determination, and some need a full Top Secret clearance with additional special access requirements. Always check the clearance requirement stated in the specific announcement; it's one of the biggest practical filters on which roles you can realistically pursue, and clearance investigations are also one of the biggest sources of hiring-timeline delay (see our [application timeline guide](/guides/usajobs-application-timeline)).

## Federal vs. contractor IT work

Both federal agencies directly and federal contractors hire heavily in this space, and the practical experience differs:

- **Direct federal IT roles** go through the standard federal hiring process and pay/grade structure (see our [GS pay scale guide](/guides/gs-pay-scale-explained)), with the job security and benefits that come with federal employment.
- **Contractor IT roles** — at companies like the ones we track on our [contractors page](/companies) — hire under normal private employment processes, often move faster, and can offer more remote flexibility in practice, though compensation structures and job security differ from direct federal employment.
