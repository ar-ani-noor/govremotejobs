If you've searched for a remote federal job recently and found the pool surprisingly small, you're not missing something — the pool genuinely shrank, sharply and recently. Here's what happened, in plain terms.

## The policy shift

In January 2025, a federal return-to-office directive instructed agencies to end most remote work arrangements and bring employees back to in-person work, with exemptions granted only where an agency head determined it necessary. Before that order, a meaningful share of the roughly 2.3 million federal workforce did some or all of their work remotely. After it, agency in-office attendance rates rose sharply — by some measures to around 90%, compared to roughly 30% just months before the shift.

## What this means in actual numbers

Fully remote federal job postings — positions with no assigned duty station at all — now number in the dozens at any given time across the entire federal government, not the thousands you might expect from a workforce that size. We pull this data directly from the official USAJOBS API, and we show you the complete current list on our [remote jobs page](/remote) — there's no larger hidden pool elsewhere; this genuinely is close to all of it.

## Telework survived, in a different form

Fully remote work and telework took different paths through this policy shift. Telework-eligible positions — jobs tied to a physical duty station that still permit some at-home work — remain far more common, numbering in the thousands. If your goal is meaningful work-from-home flexibility rather than zero in-office presence, [telework-eligible roles](/telework) are the more realistic target today.

## Where remote work is still genuinely common: contractors

Federal contractors — private companies that do government contract work, hire under normal private-sector employment law, and were never subject to the federal return-to-office order — continue to offer real remote positions, including for work that directly supports government missions. We track openings at several major federal contractors on this site; see our [contractor guides](/companies) for specifics on companies like Leidos, CACI, GDIT, and Booz Allen Hamilton.

## What this means for your job search

1. **Don't assume remote federal inventory will rebound soon.** Plan around the pool that currently exists rather than the historical one.
2. **Widen your search to telework-eligible roles** if you have any location flexibility at all — the practical opportunity is much larger there.
3. **Seriously consider federal contractors** if pure remote work matters more to you than being a direct federal employee — the mission-driven work is often similar, the hiring process is faster, and remote positions are more available.
4. **Check back regularly.** Because the fully-remote pool is thin, new postings matter disproportionately — we refresh this site daily and flag new listings as soon as they appear in the official data.

## The honest bottom line

This isn't a marketing claim we're making to sound authoritative — it's what our own daily data collection actually shows, and it's the reason this site exists in the current form it does: with remote federal work now genuinely scarce, a dedicated, accurate, daily-updated list of exactly what remains is more useful than it would have been two years ago, when remote federal postings were common enough that almost any job board could surface them.
