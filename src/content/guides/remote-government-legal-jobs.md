Government attorney positions make up a consistent, sizable category in remote and telework-eligible federal work — a meaningful share of legal work in government, from regulatory review to case preparation to policy analysis, doesn't require appearing in a specific courtroom or office every day.

## What government attorney roles actually involve

Federal attorney positions span a wide range of practice areas: litigation support, regulatory and compliance review, contract law, immigration law, labor and employment law, intellectual property, and general counsel work supporting agency operations. The specific mix depends heavily on the hiring agency — an attorney role at a regulatory agency looks very different day-to-day from one at an agency handling benefits adjudication or enforcement.

## How federal attorney hiring differs from private practice

A few things surprise attorneys coming from private practice or clerkships:

- **The application process runs through USAJOBS** like any other federal position, with the same emphasis on a detailed resume matched against specific announced qualifications — bar admission and relevant practice experience need to be documented clearly and specifically, not just referenced.
- **Grade and pay follow a somewhat different track than the standard GS scale** for many attorney positions (often the "GS" attorney schedule or an agency-specific pay system), so don't assume the [general GS pay guide](/guides/gs-pay-scale-explained) maps directly — check the specific announcement's pay information.
- **Security clearance requirements vary significantly** depending on the agency and the sensitivity of the matters you'd handle — some roles require none, others require a substantial investigation.

## What qualifications typically look like

Federal attorney postings generally require:

- A J.D. from an accredited law school
- Active bar membership (the specific jurisdiction required varies by position — some require any active state bar, others specify particular jurisdictions)
- A defined amount of relevant post-bar legal experience for anything beyond entry-level positions
- Sometimes, specific subject-matter experience relevant to the hiring agency's mission (immigration law experience for immigration-focused roles, for example)

## Why remote and telework arrangements are realistic here

Much of substantive legal work — research, writing, case review, document analysis, client and stakeholder communication — happens independently of a specific physical location. Court appearances, in-person hearings, or classified-matter handling are the more common reasons a legal role would require regular office presence, and not every government legal position involves those regularly.
